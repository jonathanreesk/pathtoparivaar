परिवार

pa · ri · vaarHindi / Marathi for family

Children need more than food, clothing and shelter. They need a home, a family, a place where they belong.

Parivaar means family. A children's home can keep a child safe, but it cannot be their family.

The best outcome for every child

Adoption, or a safe return home. Whatever is best for the child.

When a child is placed in a children's home, their story is gathered first. The authorities then decide what is best for that child: adoption, a safe return home, living with relatives or foster care. We do not make that decision. We help the authorities see the full picture, advocate for each case to keep moving, and help prepare children, caregivers and families for what comes next.

How a child's path is decided

  1. Enters a children's homeoften with little or no paperwork
  2. Story gatheredidentity, family history and records
  3. Decision madeby the Child Welfare Committee, in the child's best interest

Then, the right path for this child

  • Adoptiona new, permanent family
  • Return hometo their birth family, when it is safe
  • Kinship carewith grandparents or other relatives
  • Foster carea family's care for a time
  • Other permanent carea legal, lasting arrangement
  • One goal:a place to belong.

What we do

A child can be ready for a family and still wait for years without one.

Usually not because no one cares, but because something is in the way: a story never written down, paperwork never finished, or no family prepared. We find out what is in the way and help clear it. Decisions about each child rest with the authorities; we work alongside them and the people caring for children, and advocate for every case to move forward.

01

Know the child

We help preserve each child's story: family history, identity records, siblings and the people who matter to them, while keeping their care and wellbeing in view.

02

Clear the way

We help institutions put clear documentation in place, in line with the JJ Act, 2015 and CARA guidelines, and keep every case moving, so no child waits because a file went quiet.

See the framework →
03

Prepare everyone

Trauma-informed preparation for children, caregivers and families, before a child moves and long after they are home.

Who we are

Our story, and the heart behind the work.

We are Emily and Jonathan, a married couple and parents to three children. Our own family has grown through foster care and adoption, and caring for children who need a family has been at the center of our life together from the start. Path to Parivaar grew out of that.

We started it because we believe every child belongs in a family, and that the people already caring for children deserve support, not judgement. Our role is to come alongside them, bring focus, and help each child's path become clear.

Emily Kurapati

Child care and family preparation

Emily looks at every step from the child's side: what a child needs to feel safe, how caregivers and families can be prepared, and how the move into a family can be made gently. She brings years of hands-on care as a foster parent and teacher.

Jonathan Kurapati

Partnerships, strategy and development

Jonathan builds the relationships with government bodies and NGOs, shapes the strategy and documentation frameworks, and works to make sure the work can grow and last. He brings years of working alongside nonprofits around the world.

Love and attachment grow through consistent care and a sense of safety, not biology.

What's true

Children of every age need love and stability. Older children bond deeply too, given patience, preparation and a nurturing home.

What's true

A legally adopted child has the same rights as a biological child, including the right to inherit.

Questions

Common questions.

Where is the Path to Parivaar Foundation based?

We are based in Nashik, Maharashtra, India. Our work begins with children, institutions, and families in and around Nashik.

Do you support adoption?

We value adoption deeply; our own family has grown through it. What we hope for is the best outcome for each child. For many children that is adoption, and for others it is a safe return to their birth family or care with relatives. The decision always rests with the authorities. Our part is to make sure they have the full picture, and to advocate for each child's case to move forward without delay. Wondering about common worries? Read myths and truths about adoption.
