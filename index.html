Our Work

Five initiatives, one goal: a place to belong.

Each initiative removes one barrier between a child and a family, working alongside the people and systems already caring for children.

01

Know Their Story

Preserving each child's social and family history, identity records, siblings, cultural background, and the memories they share, so every child can understand who they are and where they came from.

02

Prepare for Family

Helping children, CCI staff, and caregivers understand foster care, kinship care, adoption, reunification, healthy attachment, and the move from institutional to family life.

03

Trauma and Transition

Trauma-informed training for the adults around a child, because difficult behavior may be communication, and children need relationships, consistency, patience, and safety.

04

Legal Identity and Permanency

Supporting identity documentation, family tracing, case records, and legal-status resolution, so no child stays in care because paperwork was never finished.

05

Prepare the Family

Preparing adoptive, foster, and kinship families for the child, not just the child for the family, with education before placement and support long after.

Documentation framework

A clear documentation framework, aligned with CARA guidelines.

We help government bodies and NGOs caring for children agree how each child's records are created, kept and passed on. Every stage of a child's journey has its documents, a person responsible, and a record that moves with the child. We bring the strategy and focus; the people caring for children own the records day to day.

  1. 01

    On admission

    The first record sets the foundation for every decision that follows.

    • Date and circumstances of admission
    • Who brought the child
    • Police station, district and taluka
    • Health at admission
    • Family and social background
  2. 02

    Clearing the way

    The legal and medical steps that decide whether a child can be placed in a family.

    • Media declaration
    • Legally free for adoption declaration
    • Medical examination report
    • Child study report
  3. 03

    Matching and placement

    Each step from referral to a family is recorded with its date and order.

    • Committee meeting with prospective adoptive parents
    • Pre-adoption foster care
    • Court adoption order
    • Birth certificate
  4. 04

    After placement

    The record stays complete long after the child goes home.

    • Post-adoption follow-up for two years
    • Records preserved for authorized root search

Not every path leads to adoption, so the framework covers every outcome.

Adoption

From legal clearance to a court order and a new birth certificate, followed by two years of post-adoption follow-up.

Restoration

Return to a parent, relative or guardian, documented with the restoration order and who the child was restored to.

Transfer

A move to another institution or district, documented with the transfer order and destination, so the record travels with the child.

Critical case events, such as a child going missing, are documented with the police intimation, FIR and legal papers they require.

How we work with an institution

  1. Map current practiceWe sit with staff and review how records are really created, kept and shared, not only what policy says.
  2. Agree the frameworkGaps are shared openly and framed around children, not blame, and every partner agrees the way forward.
  3. Define the handoversEach step is made clear: who is responsible, which documents are needed, and which move with the child.
  4. Hand over the capacityStaff are trained to keep it going, so the practice stays with the district after we step back.

Why we exist

No child should grow up in care because the system around them stalled.

Institutional care can keep a child safe, but too often a child stays for years because records are incomplete, a story was never written down, or no family was prepared to receive them.

Path to Parivaar exists to close those gaps. We help government bodies and NGOs caring for children build documentation frameworks under CARA guidelines, working alongside the people already caring for children so the capacity we help build stays with the district after we step back.

Questions

Common questions.

Where is the Path to Parivaar Foundation based?

We are based in Nashik, Maharashtra, India. Our work begins with children, institutions, and families in and around Nashik.

Is adoption the goal for every child?

No. We do not assume adoption is right for every child. Depending on what is safe, appropriate, and legally possible, the path may lead to return to birth family, kinship care, foster care, adoption, or another legally appropriate permanent arrangement.

Who do you work with?

We work with government bodies and NGOs caring for children: Child Care Institutions (CCIs), Specialised Adoption Agencies, child welfare authorities, and foster-care organisations. We help them build documentation frameworks under CARA guidelines, and we support adoptive, foster, and kinship families. We are starting in Nashik, and our hope is to walk alongside its institutions, adoption agencies and child welfare authorities.

Is Path to Parivaar a registered organisation?
