Myths and truths about adoption

What keeps children waiting is often a misunderstanding.

Many families who could welcome a child hold back because of something they have heard. Here are the myths, fears and everyday phrases we hear most in India, and what is actually true. Each myth is shown beside what is actually true.

10 common myths

What people believe, and what's true.

What people often believe, and what is actually true.

  1. 01
    Myth

    “Adopted children will never love you like biological children.”

    What's true

    Love and attachment grow through consistent care and a sense of safety, not biology.

  2. 02
    Myth

    “Bloodline is everything. Only biological children carry forward family values.”

    What's true

    Values are passed on through upbringing: what a child sees, hears and learns at home.

  3. 03
    Myth

    “Only married couples can adopt.”

    What's true

    Single women and single men can adopt in India too. A single man can adopt only a boy.

  4. 04
    Myth

    “Older children can't settle into a family.”

    What's true

    Children of every age need love and stability. Older children bond deeply too, given patience, preparation and a nurturing home.

  5. 05
    Myth

    “Adopted children have behaviour problems.”

    What's true

    Children who have known loss may need time and trauma-informed care to feel safe. With patience and support, they thrive like any child.

  6. 06
    Myth

    “Only couples who can't have children should adopt.”

    What's true

    Adoption is about giving a child a family. Many families adopt by choice, whether or not they have biological children.

  7. 07
    Myth

    “The legal process is too complicated and takes years.”

    What's true

    The steps are there to protect the child. With guidance from adoption agencies and CARA, many families complete the process in a reasonable time.

  8. 08
    Myth

    “A child's background decides their future.”

    What's true

    Nurture shapes a child deeply. A stable, loving family can change the course of a child's life.

  9. 09
    Myth

    “Adopted children can't inherit property.”

    What's true

    A legally adopted child has the same rights as a biological child, including the right to inherit.

  10. 10
    Myth

    “Adoption costs too much.”

    What's true

    There are costs, but adoption fees are set under CARA's regulations, so families know what to expect before they begin.

Behind many myths sits a real worry.

Fears, and what helps

It's natural to feel unsure. These worries have answers.

?

What will people say?

What helpsBeing open about adoption, and meeting other adoptive families, gives you and your child people who understand.

?

We don't know the child's medical history.

What helpsBefore you decide, the child's Medical Examination Report shares their health status. CARA requires it to reflect the child's actual health and to be kept up to date.

?

What if the child doesn't bond with us?

What helpsBonds grow through consistent, patient care. Preparation before placement and support afterwards make a real difference.

?

The legal process feels overwhelming.

What helpsEach step is there to protect the child. Agencies guide families through it, and CARA runs a helpline: 1800-11-1311.

?

What if the birth family comes back for the child?

What helpsA child is placed for adoption only after the Child Welfare Committee declares them legally free, following a search for their family. Once the adoption order is made, the child is legally yours in every way.

?

Will I feel like a “real” parent?

What helpsA parent is the one who loves, protects and raises a child, day after day.

?

Can we afford another child?

What helpsAdoption fees are set in advance, so you can plan for them as you would any other part of family life.

?

What if we can't give the child what they need?

What helpsYou won't do it alone. Preparation before a child arrives, and support afterwards, help families meet their child's needs.

Barriers children face

Why children wait longer than they should.

Each of these is something we can change together, one family and one conversation at a time.

  1. 01

    Strict legal procedures

    The checks that protect children can take time, and families often don't know what to expect.

  2. 02

    Preference for biological children

    Many families see adoption only as a last resort.

  3. 03

    Age and gender preferences

    Most families hope for an infant, so older children wait much longer.

  4. 04

    Lack of awareness

    Many people don't know the process, who can adopt, or what support exists.

  5. 05

    Social stigma

    Beliefs about lineage, inheritance and family honour discourage adoption.

  6. 06

    Worries about cost

    Some assume adoption, and raising a child, will be too expensive.

  7. 07

    Hesitation about older children and special needs

    Children over two, or with disabilities, are often overlooked.

  8. 08

    Misconceptions about children's homes

    Some believe children are better off in an institution than in a family.

  9. 09

    Pressure from extended family

    Relatives may discourage adoption for cultural or religious reasons.

  10. 10

    Fear of the unknown

    Worry about how a child's past might shape their future.
